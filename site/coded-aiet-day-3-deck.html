Data security 0.1

Four boxes.

Box 1
Your screen. You paste the paragraph.
Box 2
The internet. It leaves your building and your network.
Box 3
A server somewhere else. Another country, another company's control.
Box 4
A log. It may be kept. It may be reviewed by a human. It depends on the plan you are on.
A free consumer account and a company account are not the same on box 4. Ask your IT team which one you have, today.

If you would not
email it to a supplier,

One sentence. It covers almost every case, and you can remember it under pressure.
Data security 0.3

The five second scan.

Before you press paste, look for five things.
Names
A person who did not agree to this
Numbers
Account, ID, licence, passport, phone
Contracts
Anything with a signature or a price on it
Salary
Pay, bonus, headcount by person
Health
Sick leave, medical notes, insurance claims
Find one? Replace it with a placeholder and keep going. You almost never need the real value for the tool to help you.
Security 3 of 5

Never paste raw
customer data.
Anonymise first.

What is safe to type into a prompt, and what never is.
🔴The golden rule. Never paste raw personal data: civil ID numbers, account numbers, balances, salaries, or passwords. Ever.
🎭The anonymising technique. Swap real names for placeholders. "Analyse customer Khalid Al-Sabah's payment history" becomes "Analyse Customer A's payment history."
🏷️Sensitivity labels. If your organization labels files as confidential, those labels can stop a tool extracting the data at all. Ask whether yours does.
You almost never need the real value. The shape of the problem is what the tool works on.
Security 4 of 5

Why be more careful
than with Copilot?

Not because one is good and one is bad. Because of where each one sits.
Microsoft Copilot
Inside your tenant
Sits inside your company's Microsoft environment
Sees only what your account already has permission to see
Your IT team already set the boundary, so the guardrail is on by default
Your files usually never leave the tenant
A public AI tool
Outside your tenant
Sits outside your organization entirely
Sees exactly what you paste into it, and nothing else
You are the boundary. There is no IT team between you and the box.
Whatever you paste has left the building
The difference that matters: with Copilot the guardrail is configured for you. With a public tool the guardrail is your own judgment, every single time.

Use cases

Same tool.
Different rules
per department.

What is a safe productivity boost in one team is a compliance problem in another. Find yours in the next four slides.
The pattern is always the same: drafting and structuring are safe. Deciding about a named person is not.
Use case · HR

Human Resources

Safe productivity boosters
  • Draft job descriptions
  • Summarize policy manuals
  • Format interview templates
  • Turn messy notes into a structured brief
Strict guardrails
  • Employee performance scores
  • Salary and bonus data
  • Medical and sick leave records
  • Anything naming an individual employee
Ethical risk: Automation bias when filtering CVs. A human reads the shortlist and owns the call.
Use case · Finance

Finance and procurement

Safe productivity boosters
  • Explain a variance in plain English
  • Draft the commentary around a number
  • Build the formula, then check it yourself
  • Summarize a supplier contract you already have
Strict guardrails
  • Live account or card numbers
  • Unpublished results before they are released
  • Signed contract terms and pricing
  • Anything you would not email a supplier
Ethical risk: A confident wrong number looks exactly like a confident right one. Every figure gets recalculated by hand.
Use case · Ops

Operations and customer service

Safe productivity boosters
  • Summarize a long ticket thread
  • Draft a customer reply, then edit it
  • Spot patterns across anonymised tickets
  • Write the notice, the poster and the SMS
Strict guardrails
  • Customer names, numbers and addresses
  • Complaint records tied to a person
  • Anything going out without a human reading it first
  • Promises about dates the data does not support
Ethical risk: Speed makes it tempting to send without reading. Every outgoing message gets a human read.
Use case · Leadership

Management and leadership

Safe productivity boosters
  • Turn a report into a briefing
  • Pressure test your own argument
  • Prepare for a difficult conversation
  • Draft the first version of a plan
Strict guardrails
  • Restructuring or redundancy plans
  • Individual performance judgments
  • Confidential board material
  • Legal advice you would normally pay for
Ethical risk: It is fluent enough to sound like a decision. It is not one. The decision, and the accountability, stay with you.

🔌

Connectors, and MCP

A connector gives Claude permission to read one of your tools directly. MCP is the standard that makes those connections work the same way everywhere.
📁No more pasting because Claude opens the thread, the file or the calendar itself
🔐You grant it, and you can remove it because a connector is a permission, not a copy of your data
🧰It works across tools because MCP is one standard rather than one integration per app
This is the difference between a clever tab and something that sits inside your actual work.
Section 1 1.1

Read this before
you ask IT.

What a connector does
  • Reads what you already have access to
  • Asks you once, then remembers
  • Shows you what it touched
  • Stops the moment you revoke it
What it does not do
  • Give you access you did not already have
  • Make your data less confidential
  • Work without your organization allowing it
  • Replace the paste rule you wrote this morning
Everything from box 2 onward still applies. A connector changes the plumbing, not the exposure.
